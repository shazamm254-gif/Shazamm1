# The Setup — 50 Ranked Shorts Concepts (Elaborate Scam Mechanics)

The idea bank for a faceless, AI-image-driven Shorts channel about **how
elaborate scams actually work**. Niche config: `tools/niche-scams.json`.

Brainstormed at ~150 concepts, culled for repetition, weak curiosity, hard-to-
visualize subjects, and monetization risk. These are the 50 that survived,
**ranked from highest to lowest overall potential** (a blend of retention,
viral ceiling, production cheapness, and evergreen life).

## The one rule that keeps this channel monetized

Every video explains **how a victim was manipulated** — never how to run the
play. The camera sits behind the mark's eyes: what they saw, what they felt,
what they missed, and the tell that would have saved them. No step-by-step
operational detail, no tooling, no targeting advice, no naming private
individuals. The thesis of the channel is never *"look at these idiots"* — it
is always **"this would have worked on you, and here's the exact reason why."**
That framing is also the highest-retention framing available, so the safe
version and the viral version are the same version.

## How to read each entry

Sixteen fields per concept: title, hook, curiosity gap, retention driver,
emotional triggers, scores (retention / viral / AI-image difficulty /
production difficulty, all 1–10), visual count, visual style, palette,
thumbnail, search intent, evergreen score, and the ending payoff.

**Scores read like this:** retention and viral are *higher is better*. AI-image
difficulty and production difficulty are *lower is better* — a 2 means you can
make it tonight on a phone.

## 1 — "The Scam That Starts With a Wrong Number"
*Format: Internet scams · pig butchering*

- **Hook (0–3s):** *"'Sorry, wrong number.' That's it. That's the opening move of a billion-dollar scam."*
- **Curiosity gap:** How does a harmless typo from a stranger turn into somebody's entire retirement disappearing?
- **Why they watch to the end:** Because it's structured as a slow reveal of a months-long relationship, and the viewer keeps waiting for the step where it becomes obviously fake. It never arrives. The tension is that every individual step is reasonable.
- **Emotional triggers:** Dread, recognition ("I've had that text"), sympathy, betrayal, low-grade paranoia.
- **Scores:** Retention **10** · Viral **10** · AI-image difficulty **2** · Production **2** · Evergreen **9**
- **Visuals needed:** ~8
- **Visual style:** Phone-screen-first. Real-feeling message threads on a dark phone, held in a dim room. Slow push-in on the phone, then a slow pull-back that reveals the phone is one of hundreds on a desk.
- **Palette:** Near-black background, message-bubble blue and grey, one poisonous green for the fake profit numbers.
- **Thumbnail:** A wrong-number text bubble on a black screen, and beneath it, small, a bank balance of $0.00.
- **Search intent:** "pig butchering scam", "wrong number text scam", "crypto romance scam" — strong and rising.
- **Evergreen:** 9 — the delivery method changes, the shape doesn't.
- **Payoff:** The withdrawal that works. She takes out $500 to test it, it lands in her account, and *that* is the moment she decides to send everything. **The scam's most convincing feature was letting her win.**

## 2 — "Everyone on the Video Call Was Fake"
*Format: Deepfake scams*

- **Hook (0–3s):** *"He joined a video call with seven colleagues. Every single one of them was a deepfake."*
- **Curiosity gap:** A person on camera is the last thing anyone still trusts. So what happens the first time an entire meeting is synthetic?
- **Why they watch to the end:** The viewer immediately starts auditing their own defences — "I'd notice, right?" — and the video keeps quietly removing the things they'd rely on.
- **Emotional triggers:** Vertigo, awe, distrust of one's own eyes, urgency to warn someone.
- **Scores:** Retention **10** · Viral **10** · AI-image difficulty **3** · Production **2** · Evergreen **8**
- **Visuals needed:** ~7
- **Visual style:** A grid of video-call tiles, faces subtly wrong or slightly blurred/abstracted; one tile flickers into wireframe. Never a real person's likeness — use generic, non-identifiable faces or backs of heads.
- **Palette:** Corporate blue-grey UI, cold white key light, one glitch magenta.
- **Thumbnail:** A 3×3 video call grid where every tile is the same faint silhouette, and a red border.
- **Search intent:** "deepfake scam", "AI video call fraud", "Hong Kong deepfake $25 million" — high, news-driven.
- **Evergreen:** 8 — it will date slightly as the tech normalizes, but as the origin story it holds.
- **Payoff:** The tell was never visual. It was procedural — no independent callback, no second channel. **You don't beat a deepfake by looking harder. You beat it by hanging up and dialling a number you already had.**

*Format: Social engineering · psychology*

## Production notes

### Series to build from these 50
Ranking is for launch order; grouping is for the algorithm. Five runnable series:

- **"Nobody Falls For This"** — #4, #10, #26, #43, #45. Short, punchy mechanism videos. Cheapest to make, highest replay.
- **"The Setup"** — #1, #9, #20, #21, #24, #41. Slow-burn victim-perspective narratives. Best watch time, best comments.
- **"Legendary Cons"** — #7, #8, #13, #16, #17, #38, #39, #49. Period pieces. Best shares, best evergreen life.
- **"Legal But Not Honest"** — #5, #29, #30, #31, #32, #33, #48, #50. Dark patterns and design. Best debate in comments.
- **"How It Collapsed"** — #6, #15, #18, #34, #35, #36, #37. Corporate autopsies. Best for a longer 60s format and future long-form.

### The reusable 6-beat structure
Every script in this niche fits the same skeleton, which is why the channel can post daily:

1. **0–3s — The impossible fact.** State the outcome, not the setup. "Seven colleagues on the call. All fake."
2. **3–10s — Make it normal.** Show why the victim's behaviour was reasonable. This is the retention beat; skip it and the video becomes mockery.
3. **10–25s — The mechanism.** One clean idea. Never more than one.
4. **25–40s — The escalation.** The step where retreat became expensive.
5. **40–50s — The tell.** What would have broken it. Always concrete, always free to do.
6. **50–60s — The turn.** One sentence that reframes everything, ideally looping back to the hook so the video replays.

### Casting rules for AI images
- Silhouettes, hands, backs of heads, and screens carry ~80% of these videos. Avoid generated faces where possible — they date fast, cost consistency, and invite likeness problems.
- Never generate a recognizable real person. For historical figures use period objects, letters, hats and rooms instead of faces.
- Keep one lighting logic per video (single source, hard shadow). It makes a set of unrelated AI images read as one film.
- Screens beat scenes. A message thread rendered as clean, large text outperforms a photoreal scene of someone holding a phone, and takes a tenth of the time.
- Movement: slow push-in on reveals, slow pull-back on scale reveals, a hard cut on the twist. Nothing else. That is the entire camera vocabulary.

### Channel-wide palette
A consistent look is worth more than any single video. Two lanes:

- **Modern scams:** near-black background (#0B0D10), UI blue (#3B82F6), one alert red (#EF4444), one poison green (#22C55E) reserved *only* for fake money and fake profits.
- **Historical cons:** parchment (#EAE0CC), ink (#1C1917), oxblood (#7F1D1D), brass (#B08D57).

Never mix the lanes inside one video. The palette should tell the viewer which series they're in before the narration starts.

### What to avoid, always
- Step-by-step instructions, tooling, sourcing, or targeting advice. Explain the *shape* of the deception, never the build.
- Naming or depicting private individuals, victims or accused people who aren't already central to widely reported public cases.
- Bodycam, courtroom, news or any other footage you don't own. This channel needs none of it.
- Mockery. Beyond being unkind, it kills the comment section, and the comments are where this niche compounds.
