# Extreme Weather What-Ifs — Visual Pack, Aftermath Edition

> **Two visual editions exist for these scripts.** This is the **aftermath
> edition**. The [**ember edition**](VISUAL_PACK_WEATHER.md) is the warm,
> kinetic, spectacle-forward default. Neither replaces the other.

The same 5 weather scripts as [`VISUAL_PACK_WEATHER.md`](VISUAL_PACK_WEATHER.md),
re-shot in the **Death What-Ifs visual language**: cold, still, restrained, and
built on absence instead of spectacle.

## What actually changes

The death style is not a palette — the palette is the smallest part of it. The
real move is **shoot the aftermath, not the event**:

| Standard weather pack | Aftermath edition |
|---|---|
| The wall of flame | The spiral scour mark it left, seen from above |
| The wave climbing the mountainside | The razor-line where the forest stops, decades later |
| The surge bursting through a street | The high-water stain on an emptied room's wallpaper |
| Ember orange, kinetic, cuts on motion | Monitor cyan, near-still, cuts on silence |

The disaster is over in every frame. What's left is evidence. That's what makes
it read as memento mori rather than a storm-chaser reel — the weather isn't the
subject, its *residue* is.

**Read the trade-off before you commit to this** — see the bottom of the file.

## The master style suffix (append to every image prompt)

```
cinematic memento mori documentary, photorealistic, ultra-detailed, low-key
lighting, single soft light source, deep shadow, desaturated cold palette with
bleached highlights and one pale monitor-cyan accent, subtle film grain, quiet
stillness, aftermath, no people, reverent restrained mood, 8k, dark documentary
aesthetic
```

Two additions over the death suffix: **`bleached highlights`** (heat and glare
render as blank overexposure rather than orange — that's how you shoot a
heatwave cold) and **`no people`**, which does most of the emotional work here.

**Accent color:** monitor cyan (#7FE3E0), same as the death channel — instrument
readings and cold water are the only saturated things in frame.

**Aspect ratio:** Midjourney `--ar 9:16` · Flux/Leonardo/DALL·E 1080×1920 ·
Runway/Kling/Pika vertical preset.

## 1 — "The heat dome that broke Canada's thermometer"

*On-screen text: 49.6°C — THEN THE TOWN BURNED*

| # | Timing | Image prompt | Motion note |
|---|--------|--------------|-------------|
| 1 | 0–2s (hook) | A deserted small-town main street at noon, bleached almost white by glare, heavy heat shimmer, one traffic light, no people, no cars, no movement | Only the shimmer moves. Nothing else. Resist adding motion |
| 2 | 2–7s | A mercury thermometer mounted on a shaded porch wall, mercury column pinned at the very top of the scale, cold reflection in the glass, deep shadow behind | Static. A single slow push-in toward the glass |
| 3 | 7–18s | A lone brick chimney standing upright in a rectangle of fine grey ash where a house used to be, no flames, no smoke, cold overcast light | Fine ash drifts sideways in a light wind |
| 4 | 18–30s (payoff + loop) | An empty small-town street at dawn covered in a thin layer of pale ash like snowfall, footprint-free, one street sign still legible | Ash settles slowly; very slow push down the centerline; cut to black |

**Thumbnail:** A mercury thermometer pinned at the top of its scale against a
bleached white wall, one dark chimney out of focus behind it.

## The trade-off — read this before you switch

This edition is **quieter and lower-arousal by design**, and that cuts both ways:

- **What you gain:** distinctiveness and a premium, documentary feel. Weather
  Shorts are a crowded, spectacle-driven space; nobody else is shooting the
  aftermath in silence. It ages better, it doesn't look AI-generated in the
  usual way, and it pairs with a calm read instead of fighting it.
- **What you give up:** raw scroll-stopping punch in frame one. A wall of flame
  stops a thumb faster than an empty street — that is simply true, and this
  niche's demand score (6/10) leans on spectacle more than the death niche's
  does. Expect a lower hook rate and better retention among those who stay.
- **What it costs you strategically:** the two channels now look identical. I
  built the ember-orange system specifically so Extreme Weather and Death
  wouldn't read as the same channel. If you plan to run both, adopting this
  means picking a new accent for one of them — swap this pack's cyan for a pale
  sodium-white or a cold steel blue and the separation comes back.

**My recommendation:** run this as an A/B rather than a replacement. Produce
scripts 2 and 4 (the trimline and the AMOC) in this style — they're the two that
gain the most, because their subjects are *already* aftermath and instrumentation
— and keep 1, 3, and 5 in ember orange where the spectacle is the story. Compare
retention after a week and let the numbers pick the house style.
