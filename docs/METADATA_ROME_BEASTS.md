Rome had a death sentence that turned execution into entertainment. It was called damnatio ad bestias — condemnation to the beasts.

The condemned were taken into an arena and exposed to wild animals. Sometimes they were tied down. Sometimes they were forced to run. The punishment was not simply about killing them; it was meant to be seen.

Ancient sources place it among Rome's most extreme penalties, alongside crucifixion. And here is the part most people forget: the arena was not only where gladiators fought. It was where the state showed the public what happened to those it condemned.
