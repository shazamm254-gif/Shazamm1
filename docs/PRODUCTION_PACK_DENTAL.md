# Teeth Myths & Hidden History — Production Pack

All-in-one: every script's voiceover **and** its shot prompts together, so a
video can be made from this one file. Niche category added to
`tools/niche_generator.py`; generate more with
`python tools/viral_generator.py --parent dental`.

---

## Why this pack does NOT use the top-scoring angle

The generator ranks **Teeth What-Ifs at 78/100**, with Myth-Busting and Hidden
History tied at 74. This pack deliberately uses the 74-point angles instead.

**The scoring rubric has no risk dimension.** It measures hook strength, demand,
headroom, repeatability, and feasibility — nothing about policy exposure. Dental
is this toolkit's first **YMYL** ("your money or your life") category, and three
real costs are invisible to the score:

1. **Faceless is a handicap here.** In every other niche in this repo, anonymity
   costs nothing. In dental, the top creators are practising dentists whose
   credential *is* the trust signal. A faceless account dispensing dental advice
   has no standing — but history and archaeology need no credential at all.
2. **Advice content risks removal and demonetization** under YouTube's medical
   misinformation policy. History, myth-busting, and materials science don't.
3. **The content well is shallower** — evergreen is scored 7, not the 9 of cosmic
   or death. Plan roughly 8–12 weeks of strong material, not months.

Four points of score for a large reduction in risk is a good trade.

> **The hard rule for this channel:** no advice, no diagnosis, no treatment
> guidance, no product recommendations, no "how to fix." History, archaeology,
> materials science, and myth-busting only. Cite a source per video.

## Style system

```
cinematic museum specimen macro, photorealistic, extreme close-up detail, black
velvet backdrop, single cold raking key light, bone-white subject against deep
black, one pale x-ray blue accent, archival texture, shallow depth of field,
subtle film grain, natural history museum vitrine aesthetic, 8k
```

**Accent:** x-ray blue `#A8C8E8` · **Ratio:** `--ar 9:16` / 1080×1920

**Why "museum vitrine" specifically.** Teeth are one of the single worst subjects
for AI image generators — merged teeth, extra teeth, and uncanny mouths are the
classic failure mode. So the visual identity is built to route around it:
**never generate a mouth, a smile, or a face.** Every shot is a specimen, an
object, a document, or a tool on black velvet. The constraint produces the
premium look *and* dodges the failure.

## 1 — "The worm that never existed" · *Myths people still believe*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"For five thousand years, humans believed a worm lived inside aching teeth."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** 5,000 YEARS OF THE TOOTH WORM
- **VO:** "For five thousand years, humans believed a worm lived inside aching teeth. It begins on a Sumerian clay tablet — a prayer describing a worm that asked the gods to live in human gums. The idea spread to Egypt, Greece, China, and medieval Europe. Barbers 'extracted' the worm with hot wires and smoke. Nobody ever found one, because it was never there. The pain was decay. The myth outlived the Roman Empire by a thousand years."
- **END:** *"It took until 1728 for someone to finally write: there is no worm."*

| # | Time | Shot | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | Extreme macro of a Sumerian clay tablet, dense cuneiform, hard raking light picking out every wedge, black surround | Slow drift across the wedges |
| 2 | 2–7s | A medieval woodcut of a tooth with a worm coiled inside it, aged yellowed paper, ink bleed, single lamp | Slow push in on the worm illustration |
| 3 | 7–18s | Barber-surgeon instruments laid on dark cloth — hot iron wire, pelican extractor, smoke curling upward in a cold beam | Smoke rises and drifts; tools static |
| 4 | 18–30s | A single cross-sectioned molar on black velvet showing the dark decay channel running inward, x-ray blue rim light | Slow rotation revealing the empty channel; cut to black |

- **THUMBNAIL:** Macro cuneiform tablet with a woodcut tooth-worm faintly superimposed, deep black, blue rim.
- **TITLE:** For 5,000 years we blamed toothache on a worm
- **DESC:** From a Sumerian clay tablet to 18th-century Europe — the myth that outlived Rome. #dentalhistory #history #darkhistory #mythbusting #teeth #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Sumerian "Legend of the Worm" tablet; Pierre Fauchard, *Le Chirurgien Dentiste* (1728), which refuted it.

## 2 — "The tissue that can't heal" · *The strange science of enamel*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"Your enamel is the hardest thing your body makes — and the only part that can never heal."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** NO LIVING CELLS
- **VO:** "Your enamel is the hardest substance your body makes — harder than bone. It's also the only part of you that can never repair itself. Bone rebuilds. Skin closes. Your liver regrows. Enamel can't, because it contains no living cells at all. It's finished the day it forms, and physical damage to it is permanent. Your body spent its entire engineering budget making it hard, and kept nothing back for repairs."
- **END:** *"Every other part of you is still under construction. That one was finished years ago."*

| # | Time | Shot | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | Extreme macro of a tooth's enamel surface at near-microscopic scale, crystalline prism structure, cold hard light, black backdrop | Static; the texture is the hook |
| 2 | 2–7s | A bone fragment and an enamel fragment side by side on black velvet under identical x-ray blue light, museum specimen labels | Slow lateral track between the two |
| 3 | 7–18s | A hairline crack propagating slowly across a glassy crystalline mineral surface, lit from a hard raking angle | Crack advances in extreme slow motion, then stops |
| 4 | 18–30s | A single tooth alone in a glass museum vitrine, one spotlight, deep shadow all around, small handwritten label | Very slow push in on the specimen; cut to black |

- **THUMBNAIL:** Macro crystalline enamel surface with a single visible crack, x-ray blue light, black surround.
- **TITLE:** Enamel is the only part of you that can't heal
- **DESC:** Harder than bone, and completely acellular — finished the day it forms. #teeth #biology #science #humanbody #anatomy #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Enamel is ~96% mineral and acellular; unlike bone, dentin, or skin it has no regenerative cell population.

## 3 — "Waterloo teeth" · *Dental history*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"After Waterloo, dentures were made from the teeth of dead soldiers."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** WATERLOO TEETH
- **VO:** "After the Battle of Waterloo in 1815, scavengers walked the field pulling teeth from the dead. Fifty thousand casualties, most of them young and healthy — which meant fifty thousand good sets of teeth. They were boiled, sorted, and mounted into dentures sold across Europe. 'Waterloo teeth' was a selling point: buyers knew they came from young men, not from graves. The trade ran for decades, and during the American Civil War teeth were shipped to England by the barrel."
- **END:** *"People wore them for the rest of their lives — and most knew exactly where they came from."*

| # | Time | Shot | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | A 19th-century denture of ivory and hand-worked metal resting on black velvet, museum lighting, hard raking key | Static specimen shot; slow push |
| 2 | 2–7s | A muddy European battlefield at dusk after the fighting — abandoned equipment, churned earth, empty, no figures | Low fog drifts; nothing else moves |
| 3 | 7–18s | A shallow wooden tray of loose sorted teeth on an apothecary bench, cold window light, archival texture | Slow overhead track across the tray |
| 4 | 18–30s | The finished denture in a glass vitrine beside a small handwritten card label, museum spotlight, deep black | Push in until the label is almost legible; cut to black |

- **THUMBNAIL:** A 19th-century ivory denture on black velvet under one hard light, blue-white rim, heavy negative space.
- **TITLE:** Victorian dentures were made from dead soldiers' teeth
- **DESC:** After Waterloo, "young men's teeth" was a selling point. The trade ran for decades. #dentalhistory #history #darkhistory #victorian #teeth #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Documented 19th-c. trade in battlefield-sourced teeth; the "Waterloo teeth" label persisted as a mark of provenance.

## 4 — "Not wooden" · *Myths people still believe*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"George Washington's teeth weren't wooden. The truth is worse."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** NOT WOOD
- **VO:** "George Washington's teeth weren't wooden — that myth comes from how stained ivory looks up close. His dentures were built from hippopotamus ivory, brass wire, lead, and human teeth. And the ledgers at Mount Vernon record him paying enslaved people for teeth in 1784. Historians are careful here: we don't know whether those specific teeth ended up in his dentures. But the purchase is written down, in his own accounts."
- **END:** *"The wooden teeth story is easier to tell. That's probably why it stuck."*

| # | Time | Shot | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | An 18th-century denture of carved ivory with gold wire and coiled springs, black velvet, single cold key light | Static; slow rotation to catch the springs |
| 2 | 2–7s | Extreme macro of aged, stained ivory grain — dark striations that genuinely read as wood grain | Push in until the grain fills frame |
| 3 | 7–18s | An open 18th-century ledger page, quill script in iron-gall ink, one entry line catching a raking beam | Slow track down the page to the lit line |
| 4 | 18–30s | The denture in a museum display case, one spotlight, the rest of the room in darkness | Very slow pull back from the case; cut to black |

- **THUMBNAIL:** Macro of stained ivory grain that looks exactly like wood, "NOT WOOD" space at top.
- **TITLE:** George Washington's teeth weren't wooden
- **DESC:** Ivory, brass, lead — and an entry in the Mount Vernon ledgers. #history #dentalhistory #mythbusting #americanhistory #teeth #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Mount Vernon's own historians on the composition of the dentures and the documented 1784 ledger entry. State the uncertainty as written above — do not claim the purchased teeth were used.

## 5 — "Nine thousand years ago" · *Teeth as artifacts*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"Someone was drilling human teeth nine thousand years ago."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** 9,000 YEARS AGO
- **VO:** "Someone was drilling human teeth nine thousand years ago. At a Neolithic site in what's now Pakistan, archaeologists found eleven molars with perfectly round holes bored into the chewing surface — made with a flint tip spun by a bow drill, the same tool they used for beads. The holes reach the sensitive layer beneath the enamel. There was no anaesthetic. And the wear inside those holes shows the patients kept using those teeth for years afterward."
- **END:** *"Which means they sat still for it. And then they came back."*

| # | Time | Shot | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | Extreme macro of an ancient molar with a perfectly round bore hole in its chewing surface, black velvet, hard raking light | Static; the impossible neatness of the hole is the hook |
| 2 | 2–7s | A reconstructed flint-tipped bow drill on a worn workbench beside stone beads, archaeological display lighting | Bow draws back and forth slowly |
| 3 | 7–18s | A Neolithic mud-brick settlement at dawn, low walls, cooking smoke, empty of people | Smoke rises; slow drift across rooftops |
| 4 | 18–30s | The drilled molar rotating slowly on black velvet, x-ray blue light raking across the bore hole | Full slow rotation; hole passes through the light; cut to black |

- **THUMBNAIL:** Macro ancient molar with a stark round drill hole, black background, blue rim light, "9,000 YEARS" space above.
- **TITLE:** Someone drilled human teeth 9,000 years ago
- **DESC:** Eleven molars, flint bow drills, no anaesthetic — and the patients survived. #archaeology #history #dentalhistory #ancient #science #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Coppa et al., *Nature*, 2006 — drilled molar crowns from Mehrgarh, dated ~7,500–9,000 years old.

## Production notes

- **Never generate a mouth or a smile.** Specimens, tools, documents, and rooms
  only. This is where AI fails most visibly, and where a single bad frame costs
  you the channel's credibility.
- **Comment engine:** scripts 1 and 4 are correction-bait in the good way —
  people arrive to argue about the wooden teeth. Reply with the Mount Vernon
  source and the argument becomes engagement.
- **Cadence:** with evergreen scored at 7, plan 8–12 weeks of strong material
  rather than months. Pair this with a deeper niche if it becomes a main channel.
- **If you ever feel the pull to give advice** — a "you should" or a "try this" —
  that is the moment the channel changes category from history to health. Don't.
