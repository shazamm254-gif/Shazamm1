# The Setup — Thumbnail & First-Frame Checklist

For Shorts, the **first frame** *is* the thumbnail in most places — it's what
shows in the feed, on your channel grid, and in search. Treat frame 1 of every
Short as a thumbnail you have to earn the swipe with. (You can also set a custom
thumbnail for the grid and search; do both.)

Run this checklist on every upload before it goes out.

---

## The problem this niche has, and the fix

Most channels win the swipe with a **face** — a shocked expression is the most
attention-grabbing object a human can look at. This channel has no face. It has
phones, documents, screens and silhouettes, and it stays that way, because
generated faces date badly and invite likeness problems.

So the replacement is a hard rule, and it's the whole strategy:

> **A completely ordinary object, with exactly one thing wrong with it.**

A peeling corner on a QR sticker. Two cherries and a third reel a hair short.
One digit of an invoice in red. Nine identical silhouettes in a video call grid.
The viewer's eye finds the anomaly before they've read a word, and the anomaly
*is* the question. That's what replaces the face.

One thing wrong. Not two. Two anomalies read as a graphic; one reads as a clue.

---

## The 5-second test

Glance at your first frame for 5 seconds and ask:
**"Would a stranger scrolling at full speed stop for this — and would they stop
because something is off, not because it's loud?"**

If the frame is only loud, it looks like the scam ads it's about. Redo it.

---

## Must-haves (every thumbnail / first frame)

- [ ] **One ordinary object, huge in frame.** A phone, a card, a parcel, a
  letter. Not a collage, not a scene with three things happening.
- [ ] **Exactly one anomaly**, and it's the thing you'd want a stranger to
  point at. If you can't name the anomaly in a word, the frame isn't finished.
- [ ] **Near-black or parchment background.** Lane A (modern) is UI blue on
  near-black; Lane B (historical) is brass on parchment. Nothing else. The
  palette is doing brand work every time someone sees your grid.
- [ ] **Reads at fingernail size.** Shrink it. Is the object still obvious and
  the anomaly still findable? Detail dies at feed size; shape and contrast live.
- [ ] **Hands, never faces.** Hands holding a phone are the closest thing to a
  face this channel gets, and they carry a surprising amount of attention.
  Elderly hands in lamplight outperform any generated portrait.
- [ ] **Raises "how?", never answers "what".** "A gift card rack" is a topic.
  "A gift card rack and the question WHY?" is a video.
- [ ] **A specific number beats any adjective.** $25M. £0.00. 4:59. 46 seconds.
  Numbers read instantly at small size and promise a concrete story. This niche
  is full of them — use them.

---

## Text on the image

- [ ] **2–4 words max.** The feed is small and the Shorts UI is greedy.
- [ ] **One font across every Short**, white or pale, with a dark stroke so it
  survives any background.
- [ ] **Top third only.** The bottom and right edge are covered by the Shorts UI
  — title, channel handle, buttons. Anything important down there is gone.
- [ ] **Use one of the three shapes that work here:**
  - *The flat contradiction* — ON PURPOSE · ALL OF THEM · TWICE · NO BLOCKCHAIN
  - *The number* — $25 MILLION · IT NEVER ENDS · 46 SECONDS
  - *The question* — WHY GIFT CARDS? · SPOT IT · WHICH ONE IS FAKE?
  - *The unfamiliar name* — POYAIS · ONECOIN. Use sparingly, and only when
    the image explains what kind of thing the name is.
- [ ] **Don't repeat the title verbatim.** The title is already on screen. The
  on-image text is a *second* hook, not an echo.
- [ ] **Never mock the victim.** No "HOW STUPID", no "IMAGINE FALLING FOR THIS".
  It kills the comment section, it's the opposite of the channel thesis, and it
  performs worse — sympathy gets shared, contempt gets scrolled.

## The hazards specific to this niche

These are the ones that will actually hurt you. They don't apply to any of the
other niches in this repo.

- [ ] **Your thumbnail must not itself look like a scam.** A flashing red alert
  frame, a fake virus warning, a "YOUR ACCOUNT HAS BEEN SUSPENDED" banner —
  these read as the exact scareware ads viewers have trained themselves to
  ignore, and they invite reports. Use **one** red accent on a dark frame,
  never a red-flooded frame.
- [ ] **No real brand, logo, or institution name.** Not on the card, not on the
  app, not on the letterhead. A real bank's mark on a fraud thumbnail is a
  trademark problem and a defamation problem simultaneously. Invent the brand.
- [ ] **Never a usable replica.** No convincing fake login page, no scannable QR
  code, no legible fake invoice. Abstract and generic is the honest choice, and
  it reads faster at feed size anyway.
- [ ] **No real person's face — living or historical.** Objects, rooms, light,
  hands, silhouettes.
- [ ] **Skip the four dead clichés.** The hooded figure in a balaclava. Green
  matrix code. The Guy Fawkes mask. A padlock floating over a globe. Every
  low-effort scam content farm uses these, and viewers have learned to read them
  as "this video contains nothing." Your ordinary-object-with-one-flaw approach
  is the entire visual differentiation of the channel — don't throw it away for
  a stock hacker.
- [ ] **No claim you can't support.** "THE BIGGEST SCAM EVER", "THEY STOLE
  BILLIONS" on a case where that isn't documented is how a channel about
  dishonesty becomes dishonest. Use the number from your source line.

## Brand consistency (so the grid reads as a channel)

- [ ] **Same font, same text placement**, every Short.
- [ ] **Two colour families, never mixed.** Lane A: near-black + UI blue
  `#3B82F6`, one alert red `#EF4444`. Lane B: parchment `#EAE0CC` + brass
  `#B08D57`. Your grid should look like a dark case file with occasional aged
  documents in it — recognisable as yours in one glance.
- [ ] **Green means fake.** Poison green `#22C55E` is reserved channel-wide for
  fake money and fake profits. Never use it for anything genuine. Regular
  viewers will learn this, and it makes your thumbnails legible before they're
  read.
- [ ] **Optional corner badge** for the series — Nobody Falls For This / The
  Setup / Legendary Cons / Legal But Not Honest / How It Collapsed — so viewers
  learn the formats.

## Per-script thumbnail specs (top 10)

Each of these is already prompted in
[`VISUAL_PACK_SCAMS.md`](VISUAL_PACK_SCAMS.md). This is the checkable version:
the anomaly, the text, and the shape it uses.

| # | Script | The one thing wrong | On-image text | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The wrong number | A single stranded message bubble in a vast empty screen | SORRY, WRONG NUMBER | Contradiction |
| 2 | Deepfake call | Nine tiles, all the same silhouette | ALL OF THEM | Contradiction |
| 3 | Voice cloning | A phone lit for a call with a warm staircase light behind | IT WASN'T HER | Contradiction |
| 4 | Typos on purpose | One blank line circled in thick red pen | ON PURPOSE | Contradiction |
| 5 | The near-miss | The third reel stopped a hair short | ALMOST | Contradiction |
| 6 | No blockchain | A gold coin cracked open, empty inside | NO BLOCKCHAIN | Contradiction |
| 7 | Eiffel Tower | A scrap tag tied to a national monument | TWICE | Number |
| 8 | Poyais | A gold coastline on a map of nowhere | POYAIS | Name |
| 9 | Bank impersonation | A cracked padlock beside a normal call screen | "YOUR ACCOUNT IS AT RISK" | Contradiction |
| 10 | Gift cards | One card pulled forward from a wall of them | WHY GIFT CARDS? | Question |

Note that seven of ten are contradictions. That's not laziness — the flat
contradiction is the strongest shape in this niche because the whole channel is
about things not being what they appear. Rotate in numbers and questions so the
grid doesn't get monotonous, but don't force variety at the cost of the hook.

## Quick reject list (redo the frame if any are true)

- ✗ The object is small, off-centre, or competing with a second object
- ✗ Two or more anomalies — the eye doesn't know where to go
- ✗ First frame is a title card, logo, or black intro
- ✗ Text is over 4 words, or unreadable shrunk down
- ✗ Anything important sits in the bottom or far-right (UI covers it)
- ✗ The frame is red-flooded or looks like a virus warning
- ✗ A real brand, logo, or institution name is visible
- ✗ A hooded hacker, matrix code, mask, or floating padlock appears
- ✗ The text mocks the victim
- ✗ The image answers the question instead of raising it
- ✗ A number appears that isn't in your source line
- ✗ It doesn't look like the rest of your channel

---

### Workflow

1. Pick the frame containing the anomaly — usually shot 1 from the visual pack.
2. Confirm there is exactly **one** thing wrong in it. Remove the second.
3. Add 2–4 words in the channel font, top third, one of the three shapes.
4. Shrink to fingernail size. Can you still find the anomaly? If not, make the
   object bigger — never make the text bigger.
5. Set a matching custom thumbnail for the grid and search.
6. Run the title through `tools/optimize_metadata.py` and check it doesn't
   simply repeat the on-image text.
7. Check the reject list. Two of those items (real brands, victim mockery) are
   worth a second look every single time — they're the ones that slip through.

> If your account has thumbnail **Test & Compare** available, the highest-value
> thing to test in this niche is *anomaly vs. no anomaly* on the same object —
> the identical phone shot with and without the peeling sticker. That single
> comparison will tell you more about your audience than a dozen text variants.
