# Animal What-Ifs — Production Pack

All-in-one: every script's voiceover **and** its shot prompts together, so a
video can be made from this one file. Niche generated by
`tools/viral_generator.py --parent animal` — Animal Kingdom Extremes × What-If
Scenarios, **78/100 (A-TIER)**.

Every case below is real and documented — dramatized, not fabricated. Narrate
with restrained awe (~2.5 words/second). Hook in the first 2 seconds, cut every
1.5–3s, end on the loop line.

---

## ⚠️ Nature is the one niche where AI visuals are weakest

Every other niche in this toolkit is abstract enough that AI carries the visuals.
This one isn't. Viewers know exactly what an ant, a shark, and a frog look like,
so anatomy errors — extra legs, wrong joints, melted eyes — are instantly visible
and read as slop. Nobody can tell if a black hole is anatomically wrong; everyone
can tell if a shrimp has five claws.

**Split your sourcing:**

- **Licensed stock footage** for actual animal behaviour. Shots needing this are
  marked **`[STOCK]`** below. Pexels/Pixabay cover common species; Artgrid or
  Storyblocks for the rest.
- **AI generation** for environments, macro textures, and abstract physics — the
  cavitation bubble, the spore cloud, the ice crystals — where there is no
  anatomy to get wrong.

If a shot is marked `[STOCK]` and you generate it instead, that is the frame that
gets you called out in the comments.

## Style system

```
cinematic natural history macro, photorealistic, extreme close-up detail,
dark backdrop, single hard key light with soft fill, saturated subject against
desaturated ground, one acid-green accent, shallow depth of field, subtle film
grain, premium wildlife documentary aesthetic, 8k
```

**Accent:** venom green `#8CFF3D` · **Ratio:** `--ar 9:16` / 1080×1920

Deliberately distinct from the Weather channel's ember orange and the Death
channel's monitor cyan, so all three read as separate channels. The look to
chase is a black-backdrop studio macro — that is what separates premium natural
history from stock-footage filler.

## 1 — "The fungus that drives an ant" · *Venom & parasites*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"There's a fungus that takes over an ant's body and drives it like a car."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** THE DEATH GRIP
- **VO:** "There's a fungus that takes over an ant's body and drives it like a car. Ophiocordyceps infects a carpenter ant, then floods it with chemicals until it abandons its colony, climbs to a precise height, and clamps its jaws onto a leaf vein. Researchers call it the death grip. The ant dies there. Days later the fungus erupts from its head and rains spores on the colony below. Its brain is left intact — it just isn't driving anymore."
- **END:** *"Its brain was never destroyed. Only overruled."*

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | Extreme macro of an ant clamped to a leaf vein, jaws locked, a single pale fungal stalk emerging from behind its head, pure black backdrop, acid-green rim light | Absolutely still — the stillness IS the hook |
| 2 | 2–7s | **`[STOCK]`** A lone carpenter ant walking up a stem, away from a distant trail of other ants, shallow focus | Slow climb; the trail blurred far below |
| 3 | 7–18s | Macro of insect mandibles locked into the fibrous underside of a leaf vein, dew beads, forest bokeh behind | Push in until the grip fills frame |
| 4 | 18–30s | A fungal stalk fruiting, fine spores drifting down through a shaft of green forest light onto the canopy below | Spores fall through the beam; cut to black |

- **THUMBNAIL:** Macro ant silhouette clamped to a leaf with a fungal stalk rising from its head, black background, acid-green rim.
- **TITLE:** The fungus that hijacks an ant's body
- **DESC:** Ophiocordyceps doesn't kill the brain — it overrules it. The death grip, explained. #nature #natureismetal #fungi #parasites #biology #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Hughes lab (Penn State) — fungal cells surround muscle tissue while the brain remains structurally intact.

## 2 — "The punch that boils water" · *Predator vs. prey*

The water can't move out of the way, so it tears open into a vacuum bubble. When that bubble collapses it releases a flash of light and a shockwave hot enough to stun prey the strike already missed. Aquariums keep them in reinforced tanks."
- **END:** *"It hits twice. The second hit is the water."*

## 3 — "The frog that stops its own heart" · *Extreme adaptations*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"This frog freezes solid every winter. Its heart stops for months."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** NO HEARTBEAT · 3 MONTHS
- **VO:** "This frog freezes solid every winter — and its heart stops completely. When the temperature drops, the wood frog pulls water out of its cells and floods them with glucose, an antifreeze it brews from its own liver. Up to two-thirds of its body turns to ice. No heartbeat. No breathing. No brain activity. It stays that way for months. Then spring comes, it thaws from the inside out, and the heart simply starts again."
- **END:** *"By every definition we use, it was dead. It just didn't stay that way."*

| # | Time | Shot | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | **`[STOCK]`** Macro of a small frog encased in clear ice on frozen leaf litter, frost on its skin, perfectly motionless | Zero movement. Let the stillness read as wrong |
| 2 | 2–7s | Time-lapse macro of ice crystals spreading in feathered fronds across a wet organic surface, acid-green backlight through the ice | Crystals grow outward in fast time-lapse |
| 3 | 7–18s | Frozen forest floor in blue winter light, leaf litter locked in ice, absolute silence, no animals visible | Almost static; the faintest snowfall |
| 4 | 18–30s | Meltwater running over thawing leaf litter in spring light, a single amphibian eye opening in macro | Water runs; eye opens; cut to black on first movement |

- **THUMBNAIL:** A frog frozen in clear ice, macro, frost crystals catching acid-green light, black surround.
- **TITLE:** This frog stops its own heart for 3 months
- **DESC:** Two-thirds ice, no heartbeat, no brain activity — then it thaws and restarts. #nature #biology #animals #survival #science #shorts
- **SOURCE:** *Rana sylvatica* — glucose cryoprotection from liver glycogen; ~65–70% of body water freezes; cardiac arrest with spontaneous resumption on thaw.

## 4 — "Older than the country" · *Record-holding animals*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"There are sharks alive right now that were born before the United States existed."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** ~400 YEARS OLD
- **VO:** "There are sharks swimming right now that were born before the United States existed. Greenland sharks live in near-freezing Arctic water and grow about one centimeter a year. Radiocarbon dating of their eye lenses puts the oldest at around four hundred years — alive during the Salem witch trials. They don't even reproduce until they're roughly a hundred and fifty. A shark that hatched in the age of sail is still down there, still growing."
- **END:** *"It hasn't finished its first century of adulthood."*

| # | Time | Shot | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–2s | **`[STOCK]`** A very large slow shark emerging from black water toward camera, scarred pale skin, clouded eyes | Slow, heavy, unhurried approach |
| 2 | 2–7s | Underside of an Arctic ice sheet, shafts of pale green light through cracks, a dark silhouette passing far below | Silhouette drifts slowly across frame |
| 3 | 7–18s | Extreme macro of a clouded, opaque shark eye, milky lens catching a single light source, deep black around it | Static; slow push into the lens |
| 4 | 18–30s | A massive silhouette descending into total black water, disappearing gradually, no bottom visible | Fades into black naturally; cut |

- **THUMBNAIL:** A macro clouded shark eye filling half the frame against black, "~400 YEARS" space at top.
- **TITLE:** These sharks are older than the United States
- **DESC:** Radiocarbon-dated eye lenses put the oldest near 400 years. They mature at 150. #nature #sharks #ocean #biology #animals #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Nielsen et al., *Science*, 2016 — eye-lens radiocarbon dating of *Somniosus microcephalus*.

## 5 — "The animal that mates itself to death" · *Bizarre mating & survival rituals*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"This animal mates until it dies. Every male. Every year."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** EVERY MALE DIES
- **VO:** "This mouse-sized marsupial mates itself to death, and every male does it. For two weeks the antechinus breeds almost continuously — sessions lasting hours, no sleep, barely any food. Stress hormones flood its body and never switch off. Its immune system collapses, its organs begin to fail, its fur falls out in patches. By the end of the season, every single male in the population is dead."
- **END:** *"The females survive — to raise the young that killed their fathers."*

- **THUMBNAIL:** A tiny marsupial face in the dark, huge black eyes, acid-green catchlight, "EVERY MALE DIES" space beside it.
- **TITLE:** This animal mates until it literally dies

## Production notes

- **Pacing:** each VO runs 75–82 words ≈ 30–33 seconds — slightly longer than the
  death pack, because these need one extra beat of setup.
- **Tone:** unlike Death, this niche can carry energy. Restrained awe, not
  reverence — Attenborough, not eulogy.
- **The stock/AI split is the whole game.** Re-read the warning at the top before
  you generate anything.
- **Comment engine:** scripts 1 and 5 are the shareable ones ("send this to
  someone"). Scripts 2 and 4 attract "actually…" corrections — reply with the
  source line and the correction becomes engagement.
