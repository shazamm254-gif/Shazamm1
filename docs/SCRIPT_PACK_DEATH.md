## 1 — "The 900 seconds" · *The dying brain*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"We recorded a human brain in the moment it died — and nobody meant to."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** 900 SECONDS
- **VO:** "We recorded a human brain in the moment it died — and nobody meant to. An 87-year-old man was wired to an EEG for seizures when his heart stopped, leaving 900 seconds of data spanning the line between alive and not. In the thirty seconds around his final heartbeat, his brain surged with gamma waves — the same rhythm the brain uses for memory retrieval and dreaming. It's one case. One recording. But it looked like a life replaying."
- **VISUALS:** EEG trace scrolling across a dark monitor → hospital room at night, single lamp, empty chair → gamma waveform blooming into scattered fragments of old photographs → the trace flattening to a line, slow push-in, cut to black.
- **END:** *"If it's real — you get those thirty seconds too."*
- **TITLE:** We accidentally recorded a brain in the moment it died
- **DESC:** 900 seconds of EEG across the line between alive and not — and it looked like memory. Follow for more Death What-Ifs. #death #neuroscience #brain #neardeathexperience #science #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Vicente et al., *Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience*, Feb 2022 (Zemmar lab) — single-case EEG recorded during unexpected cardiac arrest.

## 2 — "The last sense to go" · *The biology of dying*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"Hearing is the last sense to shut down. They can still hear you."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** THEY CAN STILL HEAR YOU
- **VO:** "Hearing is the last sense to shut down — which means they can still hear you. Researchers in Vancouver ran EEGs on hospice patients in their final hours, people far past responding to anything. Their brains still tracked patterns of sound, the same way a healthy person's brain does. Nobody can confirm what they understood. But the signal was there, right to the end. So keep talking. Say the thing you'd regret not saying."
- **VISUALS:** Close on a hand held at a bedside → sound waves rendered as soft light entering an ear → EEG monitor picking up faint rhythmic response → wide shot of the room, someone still talking, cut to black.
- **END:** *"Say the thing you'd regret not saying."*
- **TITLE:** Hearing is the last sense to go — they can still hear you
- **DESC:** Hospice EEG studies suggest the dying brain still tracks sound. So keep talking. Follow for more Death What-Ifs. #death #hospice #science #grief #neuroscience #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Blundon, Gallagher & Ward, *Scientific Reports*, 2020 — EEG auditory response in unresponsive hospice patients.

## 3 — "Not dead until warm and dead" · *The blurry line between alive and dead*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"Her heart stopped for three hours — and she went back to work."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** 13.7°C
- **VO:** "Her heart stopped for three hours, and she went back to work. In 1999, Anna Bågenholm fell through ice in Norway and stayed trapped under it for eighty minutes. Her body cooled to 13.7 degrees Celsius. No pulse. No breathing. Clinically dead by every measure we use. But extreme cold slows the brain's hunger for oxygen — so doctors rewarmed her blood outside her body, and she came back. She's a radiologist now, at the hospital that saved her."
- **VISUALS:** Aerial of a frozen Norwegian stream, a dark break in the ice → thermometer dropping past 13.7°C → operating theatre, blood running through a rewarming circuit → a woman walking a hospital corridor in a white coat, cut to black.
- **END:** *"In emergency medicine there's a rule: you're not dead until you're warm and dead."*
- **TITLE:** Her heart stopped for 3 hours. She survived.
- **DESC:** 13.7°C — the coldest survivable body temperature ever recorded. Follow for more Death What-Ifs. #death #survival #medicine #hypothermia #science #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Gilbert et al., *The Lancet*, 2000 — Anna Bågenholm, accidental deep hypothermic cardiac arrest with full neurological recovery.

## 4 — "The last clear hour" · *Deathbed phenomena science can't explain*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"Some people with advanced dementia wake up completely — right before they die."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** TERMINAL LUCIDITY
- **VO:** "Some people with advanced dementia wake up completely — right before they die. They haven't spoken in years. Then they ask for family by name. Tell a joke. Recite a childhood address. Doctors call it terminal lucidity, and it usually arrives hours to days before death. It's been documented for centuries, and the National Institutes of Health now fund research into it. Nobody can explain how a brain that damaged suddenly works again."
- **VISUALS:** Care-home window, late afternoon light on an empty armchair → brain scan with large dark atrophied regions → the same chair, occupied, mid-conversation, hands gesturing → light going out of the room, slow push-in, cut to black.
- **END:** *"Nobody can explain it. It happens anyway."*
- **TITLE:** Dementia patients sometimes wake up right before they die
- **DESC:** Terminal lucidity: years of silence, then a final clear conversation. Science has no mechanism for it. Follow for more Death What-Ifs. #death #dementia #alzheimers #neuroscience #mystery #shorts
- **SOURCE:** Nahm & Greyson on terminal lucidity (term coined 2009); NIH "paradoxical lucidity" research workshops and funding, 2018–present.

## 5 — "The coffins with bells" · *Death rituals & customs*

- **HOOK (0–2s):** *"Victorians were so afraid of waking up buried that they patented escape hatches for coffins."*
- **ON-SCREEN:** SAFETY COFFINS
- **VO:** "Victorians were so terrified of being buried alive that they patented escape hatches for coffins. Bell pulls. Breathing tubes. Spring-loaded lids rigged to fly open at the slightest twitch. Germany built waiting mortuaries, where bodies lay with strings tied to their fingers until decay proved they were gone. And the phrase everyone quotes about this? 'Saved by the bell' has nothing to do with coffins. That one's from boxing. No safety coffin is known to have saved anybody."
- **VISUALS:** Patent-drawing style illustration of a bell-and-tube coffin → candlelit waiting mortuary, rows of bodies, strings running to a bell rack → the bell, motionless, dust settling → graveyard at dawn, cut to black.
- **END:** *"The fear was real. The rescue never came."*
- **TITLE:** Victorians patented coffins you could escape from
- **DESC:** Bell pulls, breathing tubes, and waiting mortuaries — plus the "saved by the bell" myth, busted. Follow for more Death What-Ifs. #death #history #victorian #darkhistory #mythbusting #shorts
- **SOURCE:** 18th–19th c. taphephobia; Eisenbrandt's 1843 US "life-preserving coffin" patent; German *Leichenhäuser*. "Saved by the bell" / "dead ringer" coffin etymologies are folk etymology — boxing and horse racing respectively.

## Production notes

- **Tone:** calm, unhurried, documentary. This subject does not need hype — the
  restraint *is* the style. Never let the voiceover sound excited about a death.
- **Pacing:** these VOs run 70–80 words ≈ 28–32 seconds. Trim a sentence rather
  than speeding up the read.
- **Comments engine:** scripts 2 and 4 pull personal stories out of viewers. Pin
  a comment and reply for the first hour — that's where the algorithm reads
  engagement.
- **Sourcing:** every claim above is citable. Keep the source line in your
  description or a pinned comment; in this niche, credibility is the moat.
