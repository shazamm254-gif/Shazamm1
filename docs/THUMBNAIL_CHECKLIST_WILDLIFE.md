# Wild Secrets — Thumbnail & First-Frame Checklist

For Shorts, the **first frame** *is* the thumbnail in most places — it's what
shows in the feed, on your channel grid, and in search. Treat frame 1 of every
Short as a thumbnail you have to earn the swipe with. (You can also set a custom
thumbnail that appears on the channel grid and search; do both.)

Run this checklist on every upload before it goes out.

---

## The 5-second test

Before posting, glance at your first frame for 5 seconds and ask:
**"Would a stranger scrolling at full speed stop for this?"**
If you hesitate, it's not done. The image must promise the payoff instantly.

---

## Must-haves (every thumbnail / first frame)

- [ ] **One clear subject, large in frame.** A single animal, in sharp
  focus — not a busy habitat wide shot. If a viewer can't tell what they're
  looking at in half a second, redo it.
- [ ] **The most striking visual, not a title card.** Lead with the extreme
  close-up eye, the mid-strike moment, the impossible-looking body part —
  never a logo or "Episode 4" slate.
- [ ] **High contrast.** Subject pops off the background — use shallow
  depth of field to blur the environment behind a sharp subject.
- [ ] **Reads at thumbnail size.** Shrink it to the size of a fingernail —
  is the subject still obvious? Detail disappears at feed size; silhouette,
  color, and contrast survive.
- [ ] **Curiosity, not explanation.** The image should raise a question
  ("what is that, and what is it doing?"), not fully answer it.
- [ ] **A sense of scale or stakes.** A hand, a human silhouette, or a
  size-comparison element instantly communicates "this is bigger/faster/
  stranger than you assumed."

---

## Text on the image (keep it minimal)

- [ ] **3–4 words max**, if any. The feed is small; a sentence is
  unreadable.
- [ ] **Big, bold, high-contrast font.** One font across all Shorts for
  brand recognition. White or pale text with a dark stroke/shadow reads on
  any background.
- [ ] **Top third or a clear corner.** Keep text clear of the bottom — the
  Shorts UI (title, channel, buttons) covers the lower portion and right
  edge.
- [ ] **A concrete stat or stakes phrase lands hard.** "390 YEARS OLD",
  "STOPS A HEART", "240 MPH", "FREEZES SOLID" — specific numbers and verbs
  beat vague adjectives.
- [ ] **Don't repeat the title verbatim.** The title is already shown; the
  on-image text should add a second hook, not duplicate it.

---

## Brand consistency (so your grid looks like a channel, not a pile)

- [ ] **Same font + same text style** on every Short.
- [ ] **A consistent color signature** — photorealistic, natural light, BBC
  Earth / National Geographic color grade. Your channel grid should be
  recognizable as *yours* at a glance, not generic stock wildlife photography.
- [ ] **Optional corner badge** for the pillar name (Extreme Adaptations /
  Predator vs. Prey / Venom & Parasites / Record-Holders / Mating & Survival)
  so viewers learn your formats.
- [ ] **No clutter, no watermarks, no stock-art look.** Cinematic AI
  generation is your advantage — make every frame look like a Netflix
  nature documentary still, not a text-over-photo meme.

---

## Niche-specific wins (wildlife)

- [ ] **Extreme macro or extreme action, never a mid-distance "field guide"
  shot.** The eye, the strike, the jaw, the venom drop — get close, or get
  the decisive action moment.
- [ ] **Let real texture sell realism** — skin, scales, feathers, water
  droplets, ice crystals. Ultra-detailed texture is what separates a
  premium-feeling still from a generic AI render.
- [ ] **A single dramatic light source.** Rim light, underwater shafts of
  light, golden-hour backlighting — avoid flat, evenly lit compositions.
- [ ] **The "wrong" detail sells the hook.** A frog rimed in frost, an eye
  clouded with age, a jellyfish nearly invisible in clear water — the
  specific detail that makes the fact believable and strange at once.
- [ ] **Avoid generic wildlife-documentary clichés** — a plain safari-shot
  lion or a stock "cute animal" photo looks like every other account. Make
  frame 1 a *specific*, unbelievable-but-true moment from the actual script.

---

## Quick reject list (redo the frame if any are true)

- ✗ Subject is small or off-center
- ✗ First frame is a title card, logo, or black intro
- ✗ Text is more than ~4 words or hard to read shrunk down
- ✗ Important text/subject sits in the bottom or far-right (UI covers it)
- ✗ Low contrast — subject blends into background
- ✗ The image looks like generic stock wildlife photography
- ✗ It doesn't look like the rest of your channel

---

### Workflow

1. Pick your single most arresting generated frame as frame 1 (usually the
   extreme close-up or the mid-action moment from the script's scene list).
2. Add ≤4 words of high-contrast text in your channel font, top third.
3. Shrink to fingernail size — still obvious? If not, fix contrast/size.
4. Set a matching custom thumbnail (for grid + search).
5. Confirm the title (run `tools/optimize_metadata.py`) doesn't just
   repeat the on-image text.
