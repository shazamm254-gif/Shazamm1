# The Funnel and the Email Sequence

## Why not just link straight to the offer?

You can, and for pure intent traffic you sometimes should. But direct linking
has three problems:

1. **You get one shot.** They buy now or they're gone forever.
2. **Platforms block it.** Raw hoplinks get downranked, filtered, or banned.
3. **You own nothing.** Account banned? Offer pulled? You're back to zero.

The bridge page fixes all three. It's a page *you* own that warms the visitor
up, captures the ones who aren't ready, and passes the rest through with
tracking intact.

**The honest trade-off:** a bridge page costs you some clicks — not everyone
clicks through. Ask me to run `funnel_math.py plan` with your real numbers and
it will tell you whether the list is worth it *at your opt-in rate*. Early on,
with a weak lead magnet, direct linking can genuinely win. The list wins
over months, because you buy the visitor once and sell to them many times.

**Do both:** intent traffic (already warm, ready now) → direct link.
Content traffic (cold, curious) → bridge page.

---

## The bridge page's only job

Turn a curious viewer into a **pre-sold** click. It does not sell. The offer
page sells. The bridge page does four things:

1. **Continues the video.** Same voice, same promise. "You came here from the
   video about X. Here's the detail."
2. **Pre-frames the offer.** Explain *why* the usual advice fails — so the
   sales page's argument lands on prepared ground.
3. **Sets expectations.** "It opens with a video presentation." One honest
   sentence here stops a huge amount of bounce.
4. **Captures the not-yet-ready** with a free lead magnet.

`affiliate/bridge/index.html` is built and ready — see
[`bridge/README.md`](bridge/README.md) to deploy it free from your phone.

## The lead magnet

It must solve the **first step** of their problem, completely and for free, in
under 5 minutes of their time.

Good: "The 1-page checklist I use before bed" · "The 5 ingredients to check on
any label" · "The 3-question test for whether it's X or Y"

Bad: "My free newsletter" · "10 tips" · anything that sounds like homework.

**You can make this in an hour.** Ask me: *"Write my lead magnet for [offer]"* —
a one-page PDF or a simple GitHub Pages page. It does not need to be pretty.
It needs to be immediately useful.

## The email sequence

Nine emails. Days 0–14. Written for a health-type offer as the worked example —
tell me your offer and I'll rewrite all nine in your niche and voice.

**Rules that make sequences work:**
- One idea per email. One link per email.
- Short. 150–250 words. People read on phones, standing up.
- Subject lines are lowercase and look personal, not like marketing.
- Value in at least 5 of the 9. If every email sells, they stop opening.
- The link goes to your bridge page or the offer, **always tracked**
  (`--channel seq`).

---

### Email 1 — Day 0, immediately. "here's your [thing]"

> **Subject:** here's your checklist
>
> Here it is: [LINK TO LEAD MAGNET]
>
> Quick thing before you go — most people use it wrong. They do all five at
> once, decide it "didn't work," and quit in four days.
>
> Do one. Just number 2. For a week.
>
> I'll send you the reason that one matters most tomorrow.
>
> — [Name]

*Job: deliver instantly, set up tomorrow's open.*

---

### Email 2 — Day 1. The mechanism.

> **Subject:** why number 2 matters more than the rest
>
> [Explain the actual mechanism of their problem in plain language. This is
> the most valuable email in the sequence — teach something real.]
>
> That's why [common advice] doesn't do much. It treats the symptom while
> [the mechanism] keeps running underneath.
>
> Tomorrow: the mistake almost everyone makes with this.
>
> — [Name]

*Job: credibility. No link, no ask. This email is why they keep opening.*

---

### Email 3 — Day 2. The mistake + first soft link.

> **Subject:** the mistake I made for two years
>
> [Personal story — specific, small, true. Not a rags-to-riches story. A
> small honest mistake is more believable and more persuasive.]
>
> What actually changed it was [approach, not product name].
>
> I wrote up what that looks like in practice: [LINK TO BRIDGE PAGE]
>
> (That page has an affiliate link on it — if you buy through it I get a
> commission. Costs you nothing extra.)
>
> — [Name]

*Job: first offer exposure, fully disclosed.*

---

### Email 4 — Day 4. Pure value. No link.

> **Subject:** three questions worth asking
>
> No pitch today. Three things worth checking:
>
> 1. [Specific, checkable thing]
> 2. [Specific, checkable thing]
> 3. [Specific, checkable thing]
>
> If number 3 is a yes, that's usually the one to deal with first.
>
> — [Name]

*Job: rebuild goodwill. A no-ask email after an ask email keeps open rates alive.*

---

### Email 5 — Day 6. Objection handling.

> **Subject:** "does this actually work though"
>
> Fair question. Here's the honest answer.
>
> It doesn't work for everyone. [Name who it doesn't work for — specifically.]
>
> Who it does tend to help: [specific description of their situation].
>
> If that's you, this is the part I'd look at: [LINK]
>
> — [Name]

*Job: disqualifying some people makes the rest trust you. This is often the
highest-converting email in the sequence.*

---

### Email 6 — Day 8. Social proof.

> **Subject:** what [name] wrote me
>
> [A real message, review, or comment — from the vendor's testimonials, the
> product's real reviews, or your own audience. **Never invent one.** Made-up
> testimonials are an FTC violation and a network ban.]
>
> [One sentence on why that particular detail matters.]
>
> [LINK]
>
> — [Name]

---

### Email 7 — Day 10. The comparison.

> **Subject:** the three options, honestly
>
> There are basically three ways to handle this:
>
> **1. Do nothing.** Free. Sometimes fine. Usually it drifts.
> **2. [The DIY route].** Free, works, takes [realistic time]. Here's how: [brief]
> **3. [The offer's approach].** Costs money, faster, not for everyone.
>
> I genuinely think option 2 works if you'll actually do it. Most people won't —
> that's not a character flaw, it's just what happens.
>
> If you want option 3: [LINK]
>
> — [Name]

*Job: this email sells hardest by selling least. Offering the free alternative
honestly is what makes the paid one credible.*

---

### Email 8 — Day 12. Urgency, only if real.

> **Subject:** heads up on the price
>
> [ONLY send this if there is a genuine deadline — a real vendor promotion,
> a real price change, a real bonus expiry. If there isn't one, skip this
> email entirely. Fake urgency destroys a list permanently and is an FTC
> problem.]
>
> [LINK]
>
> — [Name]

---

### Email 9 — Day 14. The close-out.

> **Subject:** last one on this
>
> I'll stop emailing about this now.
>
> Short version of the whole thing: [one-sentence summary of the mechanism].
>
> If you want the full approach: [LINK]
>
> If not, no problem — I'll keep sending the useful stuff either way.
>
> — [Name]

*Job: permission to not buy, which keeps them subscribed for the next offer.
Most of your income comes from people who didn't buy in the first two weeks.*

---

## After day 14

Move them to a **weekly broadcast**. One useful email a week, one offer
mentioned every third or fourth. This is where a list actually makes money —
the sequence is just the introduction.

Remember: **Digistore24 cookies last 180 days, ClickBank 60.** Someone who
clicked in March can still pay you in September. Keep mailing.
