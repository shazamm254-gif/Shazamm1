# 10 Shorts That Produce Clicks

Views and clicks are different skills. These ten formats are built for the
second one. Each is a **repeatable structure** — once one works in your niche,
you can make twenty more of that shape.

Worked examples below use a tinnitus/sleep health offer. Tell me your actual
offer and I'll rewrite all ten in your niche:
> *"Write these 10 scripts for [my offer]"*

---

## The rules behind all ten

1. **First 2 seconds name a symptom, not a topic.** "If your ears ring when the
   room goes quiet" — not "tinnitus facts."
2. **Over-deliver in the video.** Give away something that genuinely works.
   The click comes from trust, not from withholding.
3. **Hold back exactly one thing, and say what it is.** One open loop. Named.
4. **One CTA, spoken out loud AND on screen.** People don't read captions.
5. **Never make a medical or income claim.** See `06-COMPLIANCE.md`. Talk about
   what people *do*, not what it *cures*.
6. **Say the destination.** "Link in bio" converts worse than "the checklist is
   in my bio — it's free."

Each script is 25–40 seconds. Every one ends with the CTA.

---

### 1. The Symptom Callout
**Structure:** name the exact symptom → "here's what's actually happening" →
mechanism → one fix → the held-back thing.

> **Hook (0–2s):** "If your ears ring the second the room goes quiet, this is
> for you."
> **Body (2–25s):** "That silence isn't causing it — it's revealing it. All day
> your brain has background noise to mask the signal. At night there's nothing
> to mask it, so your brain turns up its own gain. That's why it's always
> worst at 11pm. The fastest thing that helps: don't chase silence. Give it
> something quiet to listen to — a fan, rain, anything low and constant."
> **Loop (25–32s):** "There's a second part to this that made a bigger
> difference for me, but it takes two minutes to explain. It's in the free
> checklist in my bio."
> **On screen:** `WHY IT'S WORSE AT NIGHT` → `FREE CHECKLIST IN BIO`

**Why it clicks:** you solved something real in 25 seconds. The held-back part
is credible because the given part worked.

---

### 2. The Common Advice Takedown
**Structure:** "everyone says X" → why X doesn't work → what to do instead.

> **Hook:** "Everyone tells you to just ignore it. That advice is backwards."
> **Body:** "Ignoring it means monitoring it — you can't not-think about
> something on purpose. Every time you check whether it's still there, you
> reinforce the loop. What actually works is the opposite: stop trying to
> not hear it, and start giving your attention somewhere specific."
> **Loop:** "The full method has four steps. Two of them are counter-intuitive
> enough that I put them in a free one-pager — bio."

**Why it clicks:** contrarian hooks stop the scroll, and being right earns the click.

---

### 3. The Timeline
**Structure:** "what happens at day 1 / week 1 / month 1."

> **Hook:** "What actually happens in the first 30 days, honestly."
> **Body:** "Week one: nothing. This is where most people quit. Week two:
> you notice it less, not that it's quieter — less. Week three is where it
> gets strange: you'll forget about it for an hour and then remember and it
> comes back. That's normal and it's progress."
> **Loop:** "What you do in week one decides whether week three happens. That
> part's in the bio."

**Why it clicks:** sets realistic expectations — which is rare, and reads as
honesty.

---

### 4. The Mistake List
**Structure:** "three things making it worse that you're doing right now."

> **Hook:** "Three things you're doing tonight that make it worse."
> **Body:** "One: total silence to sleep. Two: checking whether it's still
> there — that check is the loop. Three: [third]."
> **Loop:** "There's a fourth one almost nobody knows about, and it's the one
> that mattered most for me. Free checklist, bio."

**Why it clicks:** "you're doing this wrong right now" is urgent. The fourth
item is a clean, honest open loop.

---

### 5. The Question You Get Asked
**Structure:** answer a real comment or forum question, out loud.

> **Hook:** "Someone asked me if this ever actually goes away. Honest answer."
> **Body:** "For some people, yes. For a lot of people, no — but it stops
> mattering, which in practice feels the same. The goal isn't silence, it's
> not-noticing."
> **Loop:** "How you get to not-noticing is the whole thing. Bio."

**Why it clicks:** pull these questions from the intent engine in
`02-TRAFFIC-PLAYBOOK.md` — real questions, real search demand, and you already
know people are asking.

---

### 6. The Two-Type Split
**Structure:** "there are two kinds of this — most advice only helps one."

> **Hook:** "There are two versions of this problem and the advice online
> only helps one of them."
> **Body:** "If yours changes when you move your jaw or neck, that's one type.
> If it doesn't change at all, that's the other. They need completely
> different approaches, which is why generic advice keeps failing you."
> **Loop:** "Which one you are decides everything. The two-question test is in
> the bio."

**Why it clicks:** self-identification is enormously compelling. It also
pre-qualifies: people who click already believe the offer applies to them.

---

### 7. The Label Check
**Structure:** "here's what to check before you buy anything for this."

> **Hook:** "Before you spend money on any of this, check these three things."
> **Body:** "One: [specific checkable]. Two: [specific]. Three: [specific].
> If it fails any of them, skip it."
> **Loop:** "I made a card with all three so you can check in the shop. Bio, free."

**Why it clicks:** buyer-intent content. Anyone who watches to the end is
*already shopping* — the highest-converting audience you can build.

---

### 8. The Cost Breakdown
**Structure:** "here's what this actually costs, all the options."

> **Hook:** "What this actually costs, honestly — all four options."
> **Body:** "Doing nothing: free, sometimes fine. The DIY version: free, works,
> takes about six weeks of consistency. [Professional route]: [range].
> [Product category]: [range]."
> **Loop:** "I've written up exactly what the free version involves — genuinely
> free, bio."

**Why it clicks:** money content gets watched, and openly offering the free
route is what makes you credible enough to be trusted on the paid one.

---

### 9. The Before/After Story
**Structure:** a real, specific, unglamorous personal story.

> **Hook:** "Two years of this, and the thing that helped wasn't what I expected."
> **Body:** "I tried [obvious thing]. Nothing. [Second obvious thing]. Nothing.
> What actually moved it was [mechanism] — which I only found because [specific,
> small, believable reason]."
> **Loop:** "The full version is in the bio."

**Rules:** it must be **true**. Never invent a transformation, never use stock
before/after images, never imply a typical result. See `06-COMPLIANCE.md`.

---

### 10. The Myth Check
**Structure:** rapid-fire true/false.

> **Hook:** "Four things you've been told about this. Two are wrong."
> **Body:** "Caffeine makes it worse — mostly false, and here's the nuance.
> It means hearing damage — not always. [Third]. [Fourth]."
> **Loop:** "The one that surprises people most is number three. Full list, bio."

**Why it clicks:** high retention (people wait for the answer), very shareable,
and the format is endlessly repeatable.
