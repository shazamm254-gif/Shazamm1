# Compliance

Read once, properly. This protects the entire business. Affiliate accounts get
terminated for these things, and terminated accounts forfeit unpaid commissions.

---

## 1. Disclose affiliate links. Every time.

US FTC rules require it, and both networks require it in their terms. The
disclosure must be **clear, close to the link, and before the click** — not
buried in a footer.

**On a video:** say it out loud and put it on screen.
> "Heads up, the link in my bio is an affiliate link — I get a commission if
> you buy, at no extra cost to you."

**On a page:** above or next to the link. Already built into
`affiliate/bridge/index.html`.

**In an email:** in the body, next to the link.

**On Reddit/forums:** in the comment itself.
> "Full disclosure: affiliate link. Here's the non-affiliate version if you
> prefer: [link]"

**`#ad` alone in a wall of hashtags does not count.** It has to be noticeable.

Disclosure doesn't hurt conversion. On skeptical platforms it *raises* it —
it reads as confidence rather than concealment.

---

## 2. Never make health claims

This is the one that ends accounts, especially in the biggest ClickBank
category. You are not a doctor, and affiliates get held to the same standard
as the vendor.

| Never say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Cures tinnitus" | "What people with tinnitus often try" |
| "Reverses diabetes" | "How blood sugar works" |
| "Melts fat" | "What the research says about X" |
| "Doctors hate this" | (just don't) |
| "Guaranteed results" | "Results vary — this didn't work for everyone" |

**Safe framing:** talk about **mechanisms** and **what people do**, never about
**what it treats** or **what it cures**. Explain how the body works. Describe
your own experience honestly. Let the sales page make the product's claims —
that's the vendor's legal risk, not yours.

Same rule for money offers: **never promise or imply income.** "Make $5k/month"
is an FTC problem *and* a ban. "Here's how affiliate commissions work" is fine.

---

## 3. Never fabricate anything

- No invented testimonials or reviews
- No stock before/after photos presented as results
- No fake screenshots of earnings or results
- No fake scarcity ("only 3 left" when there aren't)
- No fake countdown timers
- No claiming you use a product you've never used

This is both an FTC violation and an instant network ban. It's also the fastest
way to destroy the only asset you actually have, which is people believing you.

---

## 4. Network rules that catch people out

**ClickBank**
- Don't bid on the vendor's trademarked brand name in paid search
- Don't create fake coupon/discount pages ("[Product] coupon code")
- Don't make claims the vendor's own sales page doesn't make
- Don't buy through your own link for the discount (self-referral = account closure)
- Don't email-spam. CAN-SPAM applies: real unsubscribe link, real postal
  address, no misleading subject lines
- First payout is held until the **Customer Distribution Requirement** is met:
  5+ distinct customers, 2+ payment methods. Normal. Not a scam.

**Digistore24**
- Check each vendor's individual promotion rules — they vary a lot by product
- Some vendors forbid specific traffic sources (paid search, coupon sites,
  email drops). Read the affiliate page before you promote
- Cookie is 180 days — genuinely generous, so keep mailing your list

**Both:** if the vendor has an affiliate page with approved swipe copy, use it.
It's pre-vetted language, and it's your defence if a claim is ever questioned.

---

## 5. Platform rules

| Platform | Key rule |
|---|---|
| **YouTube** | Disclose paid promotion. Affiliate links allowed in descriptions. |
| **TikTok** | No affiliate links in captions. Bio only. Aggressive enforcement — keep the money step off-platform. |
| **Instagram** | Bio links only. |
| **Reddit** | Per-subreddit rules. Read every sidebar. Most ban link-dropping outright. |
| **Facebook** | Groups have individual rules; admins ban fast. |
| **Email tools** | Most ESPs restrict direct affiliate links — link to your bridge page instead. This is another reason the bridge page exists. |

---

## 6. Practical protections

- **Use your bridge page everywhere**, not raw hoplinks. If a domain gets
  blocked, you change one file instead of every link you've ever posted.
- **Build the email list from day one.** It's the only asset a platform can't
  take from you.
- **Don't build on one platform.** Same content, four platforms, always.
- **Keep your own records.** `campaign_tracker.py` means you're not dependent
  on a dashboard you might lose access to.
- **Add a disclosure to every bio.** "Bio links may be affiliate links."

---

## The underlying principle

> Would you be comfortable if the person clicking knew exactly what you know?

If yes, you're fine. If no, don't post it — not primarily because of the
rules, but because this business runs on people trusting you enough to click,
and that's a hard thing to earn and a very easy thing to spend.
